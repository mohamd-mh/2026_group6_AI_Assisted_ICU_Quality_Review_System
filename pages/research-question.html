Research Question & Goals

Research Question

How does structuring AI-based ICU risk prediction as a modular, role-separated software workflow — rather than a single opaque model — affect the reliability, transparency, and auditability of the resulting system, compared with a single-pipeline baseline?

Engineering Problem

Quality review needs traceability

The project addresses whether AI-based ICU risk prediction should be delivered as a single opaque model, or restructured as a modular workflow with role-separated components (data validation, SIRS-based baseline scoring, weighted clinical assessment, verification with escalation, and explanation). The engineering challenge is to compare these two approaches directly — on reliability, transparency, and auditability — rather than assuming a more elaborate architecture is automatically superior.

Importance

Transparent review support

ICU quality review requires careful prioritization, explanation, handoff summary preparation, and documentation. A risk score alone is not enough. The coordinator needs traceability, confidence signals, missing-data awareness, and safe boundaries around what the AI can and cannot do.

Goals

Project Goals

  • Design and implement a modular, role-separated software workflow for ICU quality review, and systematically compare it against a single-pipeline SIRS baseline on reliability, transparency, auditability, and predictive accuracy.
  • Use a processed academic ICU dataset and exclude leakage columns from prediction inputs.
  • Train and report real supervised ML risk prediction rather than use fake AI output.
  • Provide explainability, screening, similar-case retrieval, clustering, anomaly context, recommendation support, and uncertainty estimation.
  • Prepare a Review Ticket and Prepared Review Summary for the Quality & Patient Safety Review Team.
  • Support review queue management, documentation, status, priority, notes, archiving, handoff summary, and audit trail.
  • Keep clinical claims limited to quality-review support, not diagnosis or treatment.
Expected Contribution

Contribution

The project contributes a course-level software engineering artifact that shows how structuring AI-based ICU risk prediction as a modular, role-separated workflow — and evaluating it against a single-pipeline SIRS baseline — affects the reliability, transparency, and auditability of ICU quality-review prioritization. Its contribution is not clinical validation. Its value is organizing prediction, screening, explanation, review-support recommendation, summary preparation, and smoke-test verification into a documented, auditable workflow for a human coordinator.
